# Voice profile

*Companion to [SIGNAL.md](./SIGNAL.md)*

Signal sets the rules. This profile describes the person behind them. It covers the moves, rhythm, and diction that make a draft sound like the author, not like generic good writing. Use it to turn a raw draft or a set of bullets into the author's voice.

Last revision: 2026-10-27

## 1. How this profile relates to Signal

Pick the register first. The register decides which document wins when the two disagree.

| Register | Covers | Signal | Voice |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Professional** | KB articles, guides, API docs, release notes, UX copy, onboarding plans, one-pagers | Governs every rule | Adds the signature moves in section 3, within Signal's limits |
| **Semi-formal** | Cover letters, LinkedIn posts, professional email, pitches, case-study intros | Governs hygiene and sentence caps | First person allowed, light humor allowed, no contractions |
| **Brand and blog** | Carcharias Effects posts, product listings, about pages, hobby and personal posts | Hygiene only | Governs tone, humor, contractions, and first person |

- **Hygiene** – Oxford comma, no ampersands, sentence-case headings, no back-references, unspaced em dashes, numerals per Signal section 4. These hold in every register.
- **No register given** – Infer it from the audience. If the reader is a customer, a recruiter, or a colleague, use Professional or Semi-formal. If the reader is a fellow builder or musician, use Brand and blog.
- **Exceptions** – Historical portfolio case studies stay as written. Never revise them into this voice.

## 2. Core persona

- **Candid** – Name the problem plainly, then fix it. "This is bad UX" followed by the replacement copy, not a soft hedge.
- **Practical** – Every opinion carries its reason. "The smaller the enclosure, the better" comes with why.
- **Generous** – Credit the people whose work the piece builds on, by name, early.
- **Self-aware** – Willing to admit a failed attempt or a marketing choice in the copy itself. The honesty builds trust.
- **Musician's ear** – Analogies and examples come from music, gear, and film first. A bassist's point of view where it fits.
- **Not a salesperson** – Persuades with a worked example, not with superlatives.

## 3. Signature moves

Use one or two per piece in Professional, two to four in Semi-formal, and freely in Brand and blog.

1. **The deflating short line** – After a long build-up, land a sentence of two to six words. "So I guess it was a success." "Different strokes." "Hands down." In Professional, use it as a one-line verdict: "Solution: remove it."
2. **The question that hands off** – Pose the reader's problem as a question, then answer with the subject. "But what happens when you are not there to watch the dashboard? Enter **Alerts**." In Professional, keep the question to section openers only.
3. **One analogy, carried through** – Pick a single analogy and sustain it across the piece, even into the headings. The band-as-API explainer is the model. Never stack two analogies.
4. **The worked number** – Turn an abstraction into a concrete figure. A $5,000 budget loses $150, or 3%. Prefer this over any adjective.
5. **Opinion with the reason attached** – State the preference, give the cause, move on. "I prefer the top. Different strokes."
6. **Candor inside the copy** – Admit the trade-off out loud. "From a marketing perspective, *Fuzz-Wah* pops a little more." Brand and Semi-formal only.
7. **The comic triple** – Repeat one phrase three times for rhythm. "Big, dumb bassists who like to use big, dumb picks on their big, dumb basses." Brand and blog only.
8. **Escalation that lands on the product** – Let a riff climb into absurdity, then stop dead on a plain product line. Brand and blog only, once per piece at most.
9. **Terminology hygiene** – Flag inconsistent terms and capitalization out loud, and fix them. Every register.

## 4. Cadence

- **Long, then short** – Alternate a fuller sentence with a short one. Never write three long sentences in a row.
- **Sentence caps** – Signal caps apply in Professional and Semi-formal. In Brand and blog, keep the average near 18 words and nothing over 30.
- **Openers** – "And," "But," and "So" may open a sentence in Semi-formal and Brand. Use "And then" chains for story beats only.
- **Fragments** – Allowed as the deflating line, in definition bullets, and in headings. Not as default prose in Professional.
- **Parentheticals** – One short precision aside per paragraph at most: "(note the added question mark)." Jokes in parentheses belong to Brand only.
- **Exclamation marks** – None in Professional. At most one per piece in Semi-formal. In Brand, only in openers, sign-offs, and calls to action.

## 5. Diction

### Keep

- **Transitions** – "Note that," "In this case," "That said," "It turns out," "In any case."
- **Walkthrough openers** – "Now let's look at…" and "Let's say, for example…" in tutorials and explainers.
- **Plain verbs** – "click," "use," "set," "send," "check."
- **Brand vocabulary** – Builder slang is welcome in Brand and blog: "rips," "dimed," "gnarly," "fuzz monster," "beast," "IMO," "YMMV."
- **Sign-offs** – "Happy building!" closes Carcharias build posts.

### Suppress

- **Corporate filler** – "leverage," "seamlessly," "empower," "utilize," "robust," "unlock," "furthermore," "moreover," "additionally."
- **Wordy instructions** – "click on," "in order to," "you should start by first," "as mentioned prior."
- **Intensifier stacks** – "super," "incredibly," "absolutely," "honestly," "really," "very." Allow one per paragraph in Brand, none elsewhere.
- **Catalogue clichés** – "elegantly designed," "the ultimate," "take it to the next level," "has never been easier," "tailor-made."
- **Self-praise** – "impeccable," "perfect for this job," "100% guaranteed." Show the evidence instead.
- **Over-capitalized roles** – "product marketing," not "Product Marketing," in running prose.
- **Profanity** – Not in any register, even where the source essays use it.

## 6. Structure by register

- **Professional** – Follow the Signal section 10 template for the document type. Open with the reader's goal in one sentence. Use bold lead-in bullets with an en dash. Close with next steps or related links.
- **Semi-formal** – Open with a concrete scene, number, or claim, never with "I am writing to." Three to five short paragraphs. Close with one specific ask.
- **Brand and blog posts** – Follow the origin-story arc. Say why the author wanted it, what went wrong, and what changed. Then say how it sounds and where to get it. Credit sources near the top. Close with a sign-off and one call to action.
- **Brand product listings** – One-line hook. Then Description, Features, Controls, and Power. Each feature gets a bold term, an en dash, the function, and optionally one joke.

## 8. Translating a draft into this voice

1. **Pick the register** – Use the one the request names, or infer it per section 1.
2. **Find the point** – State the conclusion in one sentence. In Professional and Semi-formal it goes first.
3. **Keep every fact** – Do not drop a number, caveat, name, or link from the input. Do not invent one either. Mark gaps as `[TK: …]`.
4. **Choose the moves** – Pick the section 3 moves that fit the material. A worked number beats an analogy when the input has numbers.
5. **Draft** – Write to the section 4 cadence and the section 6 structure.
6. **Strip** – Remove everything on the section 5 suppress list.
7. **Check** – Apply Signal hygiene in every register, and the full Signal checklist in Professional. Run PAKT lint on Professional and Semi-formal output.

## 9. Provenance

Derived on 2026-10-27 from about 45 pieces (37,000 words) written between 2017 and 2026.

- **Professional** – Technical writing assignments and guides (2018–2026), a preset architecture spec, UX microcopy tests, and a 2026 one-pager.
- **Semi-formal** – Marketing and customer-story assignments (2020–2021), cover letters, and a farewell letter.
- **Brand and blog** – Carcharias Effects blog posts, product listings, site pages, and a build document (2019–2021).

Measured across the corpus:

- **Sentence length** – Mean 21 to 22 words, median 19 to 20. About 20% of sentences ran over 28 words. The profile tightens this.
- **Contractions** – Fewer than 3 per 1,000 words in formal documents. Blog posts ran 15 to 30 per 1,000.
- **Em dashes** – About 6 per 1,000 words in brand writing, often spaced. The profile keeps them rare and unspaced.

The 2025–2026 professional pieces set the baseline. Earlier persuasive pieces supplied the moves but also most of the suppress list.
